Section 1

Understanding Group Work

Group work is when a small team of students (about six or fewer) does a task together. They must help each other and speak English by themselves (Brown, 2001). If students sit in a group but work alone, it is not real group work.

A group is like a machine. If one part stops, the whole task stops.

Three benefits

1. More speaking time. In a small group, every student talks more than in a big class.
2. Less fear. Shy students feel safe with three or four classmates. They try to speak and make mistakes.
3. More responsibility. Each student has a role. Students solve problems and need the teacher less.

Two challenges

Noise. Many students talk at the same time. Solution: give clear instructions, walk around the class, and ask for a quiet group voice.
Spanish and mistakes. Students speak Spanish and make errors. Solution: teach useful phrases first. Write common mistakes and review them later.

Steps to plan group work

Explain›Show an example›Give clear instructions›Teach useful phrases›Make groups›Give roles›Check›Monitor›Give feedback
Section 2

Mixed-Ability Classrooms

A mixed-ability classroom is a class where students are different. They have different English levels, different speeds, and different ways to learn. Most classes are like this.

Why are students different?

DifferenceWhy?
Learning needsSome students have learning problems, stress, or little time to study at home.
English levelSome students studied in other schools, took extra classes, or learn from music, games, and videos.
Learning styleSome students like pictures, some like listening, some like moving, writing, or speaking.

Three strategies to help all students

1. Different levels of tasks. Same topic, different difficulty. Some groups complete sentences. Other groups write a paragraph.
2. Roles for each student. The artist draws, the good speaker presents, the careful student writes. Change roles every task.
3. Extra help. Give sentence models, word lists, and pictures. Give hard extra questions to strong students.

Tip: put strong and weak students together, but give everyone an important job.

Section 3

Grouping Techniques

If students choose their friends, they can talk too much and not work. Random groups are better. Click each card to open it. Use only words and ideas that students already studied.

🎂 1. Birthday months

How it works: Students ask “When is your birthday?” and find classmates with the same month. If a group is too big, make two groups.

Example: Use it in a lesson about dates and months.

🧩 2. Puzzle pictures

How it works: Cut a picture into pieces. Each student takes one piece and looks for the other pieces.

Example: Use animal pictures before a lesson about animals.

🔤 3. Synonyms and antonyms

How it works: Each student has a word card. Students find words with the same or opposite meaning.

Example: big – large – huge – enormous, after a lesson about adjectives.

💬 4. Broken proverbs

How it works: Cut a proverb into three or four parts. Students walk around and find the other parts.

Example: “A bird in the hand / is worth / two in the bush” at the end of a unit.

🍎 5. Topic categories

How it works: Write items on small papers (rice, beans / soda, juice / apple, pineapple). Students join the same category.

Example: In a food unit, make groups of grains, drinks, and fruits.

Section 4

Practical Activity: “Café Creators”

Level / ageA2 (basic level), ages 12–15
ObjectiveStudents can order food and drinks politely (I’d like… / Can I have…?) and make a simple menu with prices.
MaterialsFood papers by category, big paper, markers, role cards, a poster with useful phrases.
Grouping techniqueTopic categories. Each student gets a food paper and joins the team with the same category.

Step by step

  1. Explain (5 min): Each team makes a café menu. Then they practice ordering with another team.
  2. Useful phrases (5 min): Practice “I’d like a… please.”, “How much is it?”, “Anything else?”. Write them on the board.
  3. Make groups (5 min): Give each student a paper (rice, juice, apple…). Students find their category team.
  4. Give roles: Designer, Price Manager, Writer, Speaker.
  5. Make the menu (15 min): Teams write six items with prices. The teacher walks around and helps.
  6. Role-play (10 min): Two students are waiters and two are customers. Then they change.
  7. Feedback (5 min): Say what was good. Review three common mistakes.

Adaptations

Needs more help
Menu with pictures, sentence models, and a word list.
Average level
Normal task with the phrases poster.
Needs more challenge
Add a special offer of the day and a polite complaint: “This is not my order.”
Section 5

Classroom Challenge: What Would You Do?

The problem. You have a class of 30 students. In one group of four, Ana has a very high level and does all the work. Luis has a low level and does not speak. Mia is shy and only writes. Tom speaks Spanish and distracts the group. They finish in five minutes, and the poster is only Ana’s work.

Stop and think. What would you do as the teacher?

👀 See my solution
  1. Change the task. Give each student a role they must complete: Designer, Writer, Speaker, Timekeeper. Each student presents one part.
  2. Use strengths. Ana is the “helper” and cannot do the work for others. Mia is the Writer. Luis is the Speaker with one short sentence. Tom is Materials Manager.
  3. Help Luis. Give him sentence models. Let him practice with a partner first.
  4. Control Spanish. Put useful phrases on the wall. Give points for speaking English.
  5. Monitor and give feedback. Walk around, ask each student a question, and change roles next time.
